An interdisciplinary design research lab

Intelligence isn't locked in the mind or the machine. It is enacted across bodies, artefacts, and environments.

Embodied Intelligence Labs LLP is an interdisciplinary design research lab. We work where the study of minds and machines meets the design of the systems people live with: computer and cognitive science, digital theory and the digital humanities on one side; collective intelligence, generative design and systems design on the other.

01About the lab

Research orientation.

We work from one position: intelligence, cognition and computation are not confined to the individual mind or the digital machine. They are produced in real time, across people, artefacts, software and the environments they share.

In our work, digital covers computer and cognitive science, digital theory and the digital humanities: how computation works, what it does to knowledge, and how it can be studied critically. Design covers digital, social and collective intelligence, generative design and systems design: how that understanding becomes systems, tools and artefacts. Our research projects study cognition, computation, interaction, intelligence, awareness, presence and simulation where these fields meet. We build working systems and use them as instruments of inquiry.

The lab is founded and led by Abhay Vohra, a systemic designer and design researcher with more than twenty-five years in interface and interaction design. He has been a full-time researcher since 2023 and is visiting faculty at leading design universities in India.

02Research programme

Our research.

Active · founding programme

Embodied Intelligence

Our founding programme. It studies how cognition and computation extend beyond the individual mind and the single machine. They are spread across people, artefacts, software agents and environments. The programme asks how knowledge is encoded in data, models and running systems, and how that understanding can inform computational systems modelled on collective cognition. It treats computation as a way of finding things out, not only as calculation. We build agents, models and simulations, observe how they act and what they come to “know”, and note where formal, representational accounts of machine intelligence reach their limits.

Research projects

Computing, Cognition and Intelligence

How does intelligence arise when cognition is spread across people, tools and machines? This project draws on Distributed Cognition and Collective Intelligence to study thinking as something systems do together, not something located in one head or one processor. It looks at how meaning and knowledge move through people, artefacts and computational agents. It also asks how systemic design can use that understanding, so that computational systems work with the cognitive ecologies they enter instead of replacing them.

Project publications
  • Systemic Design Through the Lens of Distributed CognitionRSD13 Proceedings, 2024 Theorises the systemic design team as a distributed cognitive system. Its shared maps and models extend thinking into the environment, and collective intelligence emerges as the team self-organises.
  • Diegetic AgentsDeveloping A practice-based study of computational agents, read through digital theory. It treats computation as a method of inquiry, not calculation, and asks whether what an agent knows is produced by its history of interaction rather than contained in its starting program.
  • Embodied Intelligence: Alternative Computing Practices for a Contingent WorldWorkshop accepted at EKSIG 2025 Proposes embodied computing as an alternative to digital symbol processing: situated computation, enacted through body and mind, that transforms a material situation, as in folding, knitting or embroidery. Participants design and perform their own embodied computing processes, which matter most in contingent situations where digital technology fails.

World Modelling and Simulation

How can computation help us model the systems we live and work within, and learn from where those models fail? This project treats modelling and simulation as research methods, not only as engineering tools. People build models and simulations of complex systems, change them and observe what happens. The aim is to externalise tacit and embodied knowledge and to show what resists formalisation. The work moves from prototyping services with people acting in place of the machine to participatory, Computational Intelligence-assisted simulations of traditional craft.

Project publications
  • From Representation to Simulation: Computation as a Mode of Generative Design ResearchRSD15 Proceedings, 2026 · with Mamta Gautam Uses Computational Intelligence to turn representations of traditional craft into formal models and live parametric simulations. Proposes a recursive framework of Representation, Model and Simulation, and Reflection, in which what a simulation fails to capture becomes material for inquiry.
  • Simulating AI-enabled Services Using a Human ElementTouchpoint Vol. 11 No. 2, 2020 Shows how service designers can prototype complex AI-enabled services by having a human perform the machine's role, using Wizard of Oz and concierge methods. Illustrated through a food-delivery service for people with special dietary needs.
